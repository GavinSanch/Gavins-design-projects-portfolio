# A9 – Pulleys

## **Objective**

-Based on Pulley Dimensions/Pulley Material, RPM, and Material of Belt determine Diameter/Circumference/Part# on McMaster for belt

-Based on forces use stress and material of shaft to determine minimum diameter of the shaft

-Based on Machineries Handbook, Pressure, Velocity, and PV Factor find a suitable material to make the bearings out of

-Based on all these three factors design a frame that can support the pulley system

## **Part 1: Belt Selection**

The pictures below show my calculations, FBD, and knowns/unknowns for determining the correct belt off of McMaster. So, some things I decided to assume before digging into the belt selection was; N=2000 RPM, 6lbf Static Tension, Steel for Pulley Material (coefficient of friction between polyurethane rubber equals 0.6), and center distance between pulleys to be 6 inches. So, once that was settled, I put down my knowns and unknowns and labeled out my FBD. From there I ran out the calculations using equation three from the slides and F1+F2=12lbf, to help me determine the higher force F1 and the lower force F2. With F1, I used FOS to obtain allowable stress which in turn allowed me to find the diameter and circumference needed to determine my belt from McMaster. After acquiring both diameter and circumference I was able to determine the correct belt from McMaster which was part#:1103N7, I left a picture at the bottom of part 1 to show what the belt looks like.

## **Part 2:  Minimum Shaft Diameter**

There was not a lot to do for part 2 which is easy to see as I fit my knowns/unknowns, FBD, and calculations all on one page, the reason I say this is because we are not dealing with transverse force or radial force since those both mostly apply to gears. Instead, here I just found the torque based the pulleys radius and the subtraction between the two forces. Then once torque was found all I needed was torsional sheer stress equation to give me a minimum diameter of 0.196 inches.

## **Part 3: Bearings**

Part 3 was not very difficult easier since now I have experience calculating the PV Factor, pressure, and velocity upon the bearing. However, I did have to make a few assumptions such as making the nominal diameter of the shaft 0.250 inches which would allow me to make the bearing length 0.250 inches as well. Once I made those assumptions, I was able to calculate P, V, and the PV factor of the bearing, and with those values and based on information of the book I landed on the same bearing material that was chosen for A8, Porous Bronze 841.
